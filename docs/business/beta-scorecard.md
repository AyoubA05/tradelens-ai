# Beta Scorecard

What the operator looks at each week, and what each number is allowed to
decide. Generated by `scripts/beta_health.py`, which reads only user ids
and derived milestone flags.

These thresholds are internal diagnostic gates. They are not market
benchmarks, not investor claims, and not something to publish.

## Metrics

| Metric | Definition | Beta decision rule |
|---|---|---|
| Landing → app | Unique public CTA visitors who reach the app host | Investigate routing before copy if the app host is unreachable |
| Account → strategy | Accounts with an active user-owned Strategy Profile | Fix onboarding if under 60% after 20 accounts |
| Account → first trade | Accounts with one complete journal entry | Interview drop-offs if under 50% after 20 accounts |
| Account → five trades | Accounts with five complete entries | Treat as habit formation, not vanity engagement |
| Account → first review | Accounts with one saved weekly review and five complete entries | Primary activation metric |
| Four-week retention | Activated users who journal again in week four | Do not set paid pricing until this can be measured |
| Contradictory records | Stored rows where result and P&L disagree | Must remain zero |

## What "complete" means

A trade counts toward these milestones only when it carries a date, an
outcome, a P&L, a setup type, and an answer to whether the trader followed
their rules. That is the same definition the product uses to decide
whether a weekly review is worth generating, so the scorecard and the
in-app guidance can never disagree.

## Privacy boundary

The report is aggregate by construction. It never contains usernames,
email addresses, journal prose, psychology text, strategy rules,
screenshots, or P&L. If a question needs per-user data to answer, it needs
an interview, not a query.

## Reading the numbers honestly

- A rate computed on fewer than 20 accounts is a hint, not a finding.
- Activation is five trades *and* a review. Either alone is not the habit
  this product is trying to build.
- A rising account count with a flat activation rate means acquisition is
  outpacing usefulness. That is a reason to stop recruiting, not to
  recruit harder.
